
# How I will Defend Freedom

Recent events have caused me to reflect more deeply on something I wrote near the end of my previous story titled “[AI and The Big Questions](./My_Big_Questions.md)”.

In answering the Big Question “What Should I Do?”, I wrote four sentences under the heading “Love Everyone Else”:

> 1. Love compels me to use my talents together with others to try to help everyone else live well and improve all our lives together.
> 
> 2. This includes foiling misguided efforts, intentional or not, that increase suffering and decrease joy.
> 
> 3. I believe I can help others the most when I apply my talents in:  
> - defending freedom (e.g., public sector), … \[and several other bullets\]
> 
> 4. I spend time helping humanity (including myself and my core family) through my vocation/career.

### The challenge of engaging in political discourse today

Given the fraught political environment and my responsibilities to those who depend on me, including those who employ my services and on whom I depend for my livelihood, I must be thoughtful about what I say. While that’s always been true for anyone in leadership, the risk is greater right now. Frankly, I have no interest in writing a “Jerry Maguire” letter that may help others but ends up hurting me and my family — even if it ends up only hurting in the short term.

It is comforting to know that a great many of my peers are similarly struggling to reconcile their desire to engage in the discourse of the day, as is their First Amendment right, with their responsibility to exercise restraint. I have noticed on LinkedIn an uptick of political discussion threads among my connections for example, and know that it would be irresponsible to engage in them despite the temptation — the First Amendment does not of course prevent organizations from distancing themselves when speech harms their livelihoods.

### The need to engage in political discourse in a protected way

Yet it is very important to me personally — and to my personal brand — that I do find a _protected_ way to engage publicly beyond what I say and do privately. I want it known that my restraint does not mean I don’t care or I am neutral. As I have already stated — “I believe I can help others the most when I apply my talents in defending freedom \[among other things\]”. And I want to be clear and unambiguous about what this means to me and what others should expect from me.

### The civic duty of U.S. Citizens under the Constitution

For this I turn to my civic duty as a U.S. Citizen under the U.S. Constitution. In the late 1700’s, having waged and won the War of Independence to advance their mutual interests spelled out clearly in the Declaration of Independence, the People of the United States of America, who became its first Citizens, ordained and established the Constitution of the United States, as is written in its Preamble, “in Order to form a more perfect Union, establish Justice, insure domestic Tranquility, provide for the common defence, promote the general Welfare, and secure the Blessings of Liberty to ourselves and our Posterity”.

The remainder of the Constitution’s 7 original Articles and 27 subsequent Amendments constitute the “supreme Law” of the United States as ordained and established by the People. Beginning with the Constitution’s ratification, the Citizens of the United States have succeeded in helping each U.S. President fulfill that Office’s oath to “…preserve, protect and defend the Constitution of the United States…” — and as such the Constitution remains the “supreme Law of the Land” today.

### My civic duty as a U.S. Citizen

Here in the present day, I am a natural born U.S. Citizen who firmly believes it is also in my interest to help “form a more perfect Union, establish Justice, insure domestic Tranquility, provide for the common defence, promote the general Welfare, and secure the Blessings of Liberty to ourselves \[including myself, my family, and all Citizens\] and our Posterity”. As Citizens of the United States have done since the Constitution was established, I too want to succeed in helping our President fulfill that Office’s Constitutional oath to “…preserve, protect and defend the Constitution of the United States…”

### I will help to support and defend the U.S. Constitution…

When I was commissioned by the President as a U.S. Army Officer who went on to serve honorably, I was “bound by oath…to support this Constitution…”, having taken the oath prescribed by Title 5 U.S. Code 3331, which was “I, Richard A Reba, Jr, do solemnly swear (or affirm) that I will support and defend the Constitution of the United States against all enemies, foreign and domestic; that I will bear true faith and allegiance to the same; that I take this obligation freely, without any mental reservation or purpose of evasion; and that I will well and faithfully discharge the duties of the office on which I am about to enter. So help me God.”

As a commissioned Officer then and a private Citizen today, I will continue to help our President fulfill that Office’s Constitutional oath to “…preserve, protect and defend the Constitution of the United States…” by “supporting and defending the Constitution of the United States against all enemies, foreign and domestic”, just as the Citizens of the United States have done since they ordained and established the U.S. Constitution in the late 1700’s.

Then as now, I would lay down my life, if necessary, “supporting and defending the Constitution against all enemies foreign and domestic.” As Patrick Henry once said, “Give me Liberty, or give me death!”

### …because the Constitution continues to advance our interests

I will do this because the Citizens of the United States continue supporting the Constitution as the “supreme Law of the Land”, and because I personally believe the Constitution still faithfully advances the mutual interests We the People and our Posterity all share — interests spelled out clearly first in the Declaration of Independence and subsequently in the Preamble to our Constitution.

As U.S. Citizens, defending freedom by “supporting and defending the Constitution against all enemies foreign and domestic” is our civic duty, and for me remains one of the best answers to the Big Question: “What Should I Do?”
