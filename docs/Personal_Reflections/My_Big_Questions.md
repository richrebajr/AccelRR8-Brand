
# AI and the Big Questions

Artificial Intelligence itself, and by extension the AI-enabled exponential accelerators I spoke about in [my last story](./My_Signature_Story.md), both inspires and _terrifies_ me. In this I am not alone, which is reassuring. The realization that Artificial General Intelligence could go rogue or fall into the wrong hands in just a few years (months?) has caused me to reflect deeply on my Big Philosophical Questions: Who Am I? What Should I Do?

Because I want to help the world’s leaders use AI-enabled exponential accelerators to help themselves _AND_ _humanity_, I want to be very transparent about _WHO_ I am and _WHY_ I am doing this_._ To this end, I’ve decided to share my philosophy about life, which I felt prompted to develop carefully and precisely because of the looming AI threat.

Sharing something so deeply personal leaves me feeling vulnerable. However, I know that I myself wonder about — and worry about — the motivations driving the industry’s AI leaders today. Have they taken the time to orient themselves ahead of the challenges on the near horizon? Who are they really? And why are they doing what they are doing?

## **Who Am I?**

### **What I Value — Love**

For starters, I value simplicity; as Einstein said paraphrasing Occam’s Razor: “Everything should be as simple as possible, but no simpler”. So the answers to these Big Questions shouldn’t be — and aren’t — complicated.

I was raised Catholic, but I’m open-minded about spirituality. That being said, two quotes from the New Testament stand out for me as the simplest explanation of what I value.

- The first quote is from Matthew where Jesus declared the Great Commandment. When asked which of the commandments are the greatest, Jesus answered that there are two. The first is love God. The second is love all people: your neighbors, and yourself.
- The second quote is from John which simply states that “God is love”.

These quotes spiritually validate what by nature I value the most: love.

### **My Purpose — To Love**

My purpose, and thus what I do, is love — in alignment with the Great Commandment:

- God (God is love itself),
- myself, (I consider my core family a part of myself since my joy and suffering are so closely intertwined with theirs)
- my fellow souls,
- and all the rest of God’s works (everything else).

### **What I Believe — Love Forever**

While my soul exists, I am on a pilgrimage to heaven / bliss, where I will find all who I love, who will in turn find everyone they love, and so on. Thus, I will find everyone there, and together we will all experience love for as long as we exist.

Since I know where I’m going, I can enjoy the journey — there’s no rush. If I cease to exist, I won’t know if I never arrive at my destination. If not, I’ll always have hope of arriving one day.

Love compels me to try to help others enjoy their journey. I believe based on my experiences that love compels others in the same way.

Thus, I seek opportunities for myself (including core family) and others to live well and improve our lives, increasing our joy and decreasing our suffering. Whenever possible, I avoid trade-offs that help others but leave my family worse off.

## What Should I Do?

### **Love God (God is Love itself) and all God’s works**

I like to reflect on what I value the most: God (love) and all God’s works (people and everything else). In my view this is “praying”.

### **Love Myself (I consider my Core Family part of Myself)**

I and my core family seek opportunities to live well and improve our lives by increasing our joy and decreasing our suffering, to the point of organizing our to-do list along these lines:

- We give our self-esteem TLC: What do we think of ourselves? How can we celebrate what we like a lot? How can we improve what we like less?
- We enjoy our lives through Spirituality, Romance, Fun, and Comfort
- We ensure resilience of our Lives and Abilities, Health and Fitness, Money (enough to enjoy life and be resilient), and Property.

### **Love Everyone Else**

Love compels me to use my talents together with others to try to help everyone else live well and improve all our lives together. This includes foiling misguided efforts, intentional or not, that increase suffering and decrease joy.

I believe I can help others the most when I apply my talents in:  
- defending freedom (e.g., public sector),  
- living well and healthy (e.g., healthcare) with means (e.g., finance),  
- preparing humanity for the future (e.g., education), and  
- enjoying life (philosophy, art, travel, recreation, etc.)

I spend time helping humanity (including myself and my core family) through my vocation/career.
